Our Story

Founded on a conviction that storytelling transforms communities.

Background & inspiration

It began with a book.

Virtue Literacy Africa was founded on a single conviction: that storytelling has the power to transform communities. In 2024, Virtue published Place Called Kibera — a documented account of life inside one of Africa's most densely populated informal settlements.

The book captures, with honesty, the daily realities of poor infrastructure, overcrowded housing, disease, deprivation, and systemic neglect faced by hundreds of thousands of residents.

Building on this foundation, Virtue Literacy Africa intends to expand its social mandate — not only continuing to document similar struggles across Kenya and the broader African continent, but actively working to create change within the communities it documents.

About Place Called Kibera

This landmark publication brought to light the lived experiences of Kibera's residents — giving voice to a community often overlooked by mainstream narratives. It now serves as the cornerstone of Virtue's social documentation mission.

The problem

Compounding disadvantages, from an early age.

Children growing up in informal settlements across Africa face compounding disadvantages that limit their potential from an early age:

  • Severely limited access to books, reading materials, and enriching educational content.
  • Absence of safe, stimulating spaces for intellectual growth and creative expression.
  • Little to no exposure to values-based education that builds character and resilience.
  • Disconnection from emerging technologies and the opportunities they present.
  • Lack of platforms where children can be recognised, celebrated, and supported to lead.
  • Near-total absence of civic education — leaving children unaware of their rights, their responsibilities, and their power to shape the communities they live in.

Without deliberate intervention, these gaps perpetuate cycles of poverty, exclusion, and civic disengagement. Virtue Literacy Africa believes a literate, civically aware, and technology-empowered child is the most powerful agent of community transformation.

Vision & mission

What we are working toward.

Vision

An Africa where every child, regardless of where they were born, has access to books, knowledge, and the tools to shape their future.

Mission

To document Africa's informal settlement realities and respond with mobile libraries, literacy events, civic education, values-based books, and AI learning fellowships — nurturing children who are not only literate, but informed, empowered, and ready to lead.

Who we serve

Target beneficiaries.

The initiative directly targets:

  • Children aged 5–18 residing in informal settlements across Kenya and East Africa.
  • Parents and caregivers seeking learning resources and support for their children.
  • Youth community workers and educators within informal settlements.
  • Children with disabilities and developmental delays who are often excluded from mainstream learning programmes.

Phase 1 will focus on Nairobi (Kibera, Mathare, Korogocho), with expansion to Mombasa, Kisumu, and cross-border to Uganda and Tanzania in Phase 2.

Implementation approach

A phased rollout, from Nairobi outward.

Phase Timeline Geography Key deliverables
Phase 1 Year 1 Nairobi (Kibera, Mathare, Korogocho) 2 mobile library units; 4 events; 1 fellowship cohort; 2 books published
Phase 2 Year 2–3 Mombasa, Kisumu + 1 cross-border city 5 mobile units; quarterly events; expanded fellowship programme
Phase 3 Year 4–5 Pan-African rollout Network of community library hubs; annual AI Fellowship Summit
