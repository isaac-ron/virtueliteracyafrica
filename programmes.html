Pillar i

Documentation, Publishing & Inclusive Books

Publishing is the thread that runs through everything we do.

At the heart of Virtue Literacy Africa is the book — from documenting the raw realities of informal settlements to crafting stories that nurture values, courage, and active citizenship. Virtue commissions and produces books across four distinct categories, working with local illustrators, developmental specialists, civic educators, and community storytellers.

Four book categories

i.

Documentation Books

Books like Place Called Kibera that document the struggles, deprivation, and resilience of communities in informal settlements — creating a permanent, dignified record of lived African experience.

ii.

Values-Based Children's Books

Well-illustrated books that teach integrity, empathy, perseverance, and purpose — equipping children with the inner qualities necessary for a thriving, dignified future.

iii.

Civic Education Books

Age-appropriate books that explain rights, community governance, accountability, and citizenship — teaching children in informal settlements that they belong in public life and have a voice in it.

iv.

Accessible & Inclusive Books

Purpose-designed books for children with disabilities and developmental delays — featuring simplified language, high-contrast illustrations, tactile cues, and formats adapted for diverse learning needs.

Publishing goals

  • Expand documentation to at least 3 new cities and settlements per year.
  • Develop a civic education book series contextualised to Kenya and East African governance structures.
  • Build a growing library of inclusive and accessible books co-developed with specialists.
  • Ensure all children's books reflect African settings, languages, and lived realities.
Pillar ii

Mobile Children's Libraries

A great book is only transformative when it reaches the child who needs it.

Virtue Literacy Africa deploys mobile library units — roving, equipped vehicles or portable setups — that bring curated reading materials directly into informal settlements on a regular schedule.

  • Mobile units stocked with values-based books, documentation titles, civic education materials, and inclusive reading resources for children with disabilities.
  • Fixed rotation schedules ensuring consistent, predictable access for communities.
  • Reading partnerships established with local schools, youth centres, and community groups.
  • Readership and literacy outcomes tracked and reported across all target communities.
Pillar iii

Reading, Storytelling, Civic Education & Enrichment Events

Spaces where children gather, read, debate, compete, grow, and are celebrated — with civic education woven into every event.

  • Facilitated reading sessions and guided storytelling circles.
  • Writing and storytelling competitions where children compete on essays, poetry, and original narratives.
  • Civic education sessions covering children's rights, community governance, accountability, and what it means to be an active citizen — delivered in age-appropriate, story-driven formats.
  • Know Your Rights workshops helping children understand the Constitution, county government structures, and how to engage local leaders.
  • Community dialogue sessions where children identify a problem in their settlement and practise articulating it — linking literacy directly to civic voice.
  • Light refreshments provided to all participating children.
  • On-site counselling and mentorship by trained youth workers.
  • Awards and public recognition for standout performers at every event.
Literacy + civic awareness = active citizens

Virtue Literacy Africa holds a firm belief: literacy without civic awareness produces readers; literacy with civic awareness produces citizens. A child who can write a compelling essay can also write a letter to their ward representative; a child who can tell a story can also speak at a community baraza.

Pillar iv

AI Learning Fellowships & Civic Leadership

A programme for the most outstanding young people identified through our enrichment events and writing competitions — bridging literacy with technology, and curiosity with community action.

Fellowship winners will

  • Receive structured training in AI literacy, critical thinking, civic problem-solving, and community organising.
  • Develop an original project aimed at solving a specific governance, infrastructure, health, or education challenge in their home community.
  • Engage community members, local leaders, and relevant authorities as part of their project development — practising civic participation in real time.
  • Present their project to a panel of mentors, community leaders, government representatives, and industry partners.
  • Win a funded fellowship to pilot and scale their community improvement initiative.
The fellowship philosophy

We are not looking for the cleverest child — we are looking for the most determined. The fellowship rewards curiosity, courage, and commitment to community. A child who grows up in Korogocho and chooses to fix its drainage system, improve its school, or advocate for its residents is not just a fellow — they are a citizen leader.

Pillar v

Inclusive Development & the Mwangaza AI Programme

Virtue Literacy Africa's commitment to inclusion does not end at the bookshelf.

Pillar 5 is dedicated entirely to children with developmental delays and disabilities — and to the caregivers, health workers, and teachers who walk alongside them every day. It brings together two tightly integrated workstreams.

Workstream A — Books for Access, Inclusion & Disability Rights

  • Books developed for children with visual impairments, hearing loss, cognitive delays, autism spectrum conditions, and physical disabilities.
  • Formats include large-print, high-contrast, tactile, and simplified-text editions developed with occupational therapists and child development specialists.
  • Stories feature characters and communities that reflect the lived experiences of children with disabilities in African contexts — centering dignity, not pity.
  • A dedicated disability rights series teaching children about the right to education, inclusion, reasonable accommodation, and community participation under Kenyan law and the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities.
  • All inclusive and rights-based titles distributed through the mobile library units and enrichment events, ensuring they reach children directly within their communities.

Workstream B — Mwangaza AI: Early Detection & Care Pathways

Mwangaza — meaning 'light' in Swahili — is Virtue Literacy Africa's offline-capable AI programme built for the frontline: the mothers, community health workers, teachers, and caregivers who are often the first to notice when a child is struggling, but the last to receive the tools and guidance to act.

  • Early detection tools covering motor, speech, social, cognitive, and sensory domains — guiding frontline users through structured, culturally appropriate observation frameworks.
  • Health pathway navigation connecting identified concerns to the nearest appropriate services, referral networks, and community-based support systems.
  • Caregiver coaching modules offering practical, contextualised guidance for supporting children with specific developmental needs at home.
  • Teacher support resources helping educators adapt instruction and classroom environments for children with special needs within mainstream settings.
  • Fully offline-first design ensuring the platform works reliably in the low-connectivity environments common across informal settlements.
The Mwangaza promise

A child in Mathare or Korogocho with a developmental delay deserves the same chance at early intervention, the right diagnosis, and a dignified life as any other child. Mwangaza AI exists to make that possible — not someday, but now.
